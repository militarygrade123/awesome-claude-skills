# Creative Brief

Create or refine creative briefs that connect discovery to execution, translating vague concepts into actionable documents for designers, developers, or AI agents.

## When to Use This Skill

- Starting new projects (websites, apps, brands, campaigns)
- Kicking off redesigns or rebrands
- Handing work to creative or technical teams
- Aligning drifting teams around direction
- Converting vague ideas into buildable specifications

## When NOT to Use This Skill

- One-off design questions (use design-standards skill if available)
- Creating PRDs or dev tickets (use product management tools)
- Initial research phase (conduct brand discovery first)
- Copy-writing focused work (use dedicated content creation tools)
- Deep aesthetic direction needed (use creative direction skill if available)

## Required Information

To create an effective creative brief, gather answers to ten core questions:

1. **What is the project?** - Clear project definition
2. **Why does it exist?** - What problem does it solve?
3. **Who is the audience?** - Target users or customers
4. **What should they do, feel, or know?** - Desired outcomes
5. **What is the personality/voice?** - Brand voice and tone
6. **What is the visual direction?** - Design aesthetic guidance
7. **What is in scope; what is out?** - Project boundaries
8. **What are the constraints?** - Time, budget, technical, brand limitations
9. **What defines success?** - Measurable objectives
10. **Who approves the work?** - Decision makers and approval process

## Brief Structure

Generate creative briefs with these 10 essential sections:

### 1. Snapshot
One-paragraph overview covering what, who, why, and timeline. This should be readable by anyone and provide complete project context at a glance.

### 2. Audience
Define the primary audience first—who they are, their goals, current blockers, and where to reach them. Include:
- Demographics and psychographics
- User goals and motivations
- Current pain points or obstacles
- Where they can be reached (platforms, channels)

### 3. Objectives
Success metrics paired with measurable signals. Each objective should include:
- Clear metric (e.g., "3% conversion rate")
- Timeframe (e.g., "within 30 days")
- How it will be measured

### 4. Key Message
Single sentence capturing the essential takeaway for visitors. This is the one thing the audience should remember or understand.

### 5. Voice and Tone
Three to five adjectives paired with contrasts to clarify meaning:
- Example: "confident, not arrogant"
- Example: "friendly, not overly casual"
- Example: "expert, not condescending"

### 6. Visual Direction
Mood, color palette, typography, and imagery guidance with reference URLs:
- Overall mood or aesthetic (e.g., "clean and modern", "bold and energetic")
- Color palette suggestions
- Typography guidance (serif vs. sans-serif, display vs. body)
- Imagery style with reference links

### 7. Scope and Deliverables
Specific components being built, not generic category names:
- List concrete deliverables (e.g., "Homepage with hero section, 3 feature blocks, and CTA")
- Avoid vague terms like "website" or "brand identity"
- Include file formats, dimensions, or specifications where relevant

### 8. Constraints
Time, budget, technical requirements, brand standards, and compliance needs:
- Timeline and key milestones
- Budget limitations
- Technical constraints (platform, browser support, accessibility requirements)
- Brand guidelines that must be followed
- Compliance or regulatory requirements

### 9. Inspiration and Competitors
- **Aspirational references**: 3-5 examples to inspire or emulate
- **Differentiation targets**: 3-5 competitors to differentiate from
- Include URLs and specific callouts (e.g., "like Site X's navigation, but more colorful")

### 10. Approval
Who signs off, what artifact triggers approval, and escalation if deadlines pass:
- Primary approver(s) and their role
- What they're approving (e.g., "final mockups", "working prototype")
- Approval deadline
- Escalation path if approval is delayed

## Common Failure Patterns to Avoid

When creating briefs, avoid these common pitfalls:

- **Vague brand comparisons**: Don't say "like Apple" without specificity. Instead: "like Apple's product pages—clean white space, large product imagery, minimal copy"
- **Demographic-only audience**: Don't stop at "25-35 year olds". Include goals, behaviors, and contexts
- **Unmeasurable success criteria**: Replace "increase engagement" with "increase average session duration to 3 minutes"
- **Generic aesthetic language**: Replace "modern and clean" with references, color codes, and example URLs
- **Unfocused context**: Don't include background information that doesn't inform decisions

## Output Format

Output creative briefs as markdown files (`creative-brief.md`) under 1,500 words. For large projects, include linked appendices for additional detail rather than bloating the main brief.

## How to Use

### Basic Usage

Ask Claude to create a creative brief by providing project context:

```
Create a creative brief for a new e-commerce website selling sustainable fashion to millennials.
```

### Guided Creation

If information is incomplete, Claude will ask clarifying questions based on the 10 required questions listed above. Answer them to build a comprehensive brief.

### Refinement

Provide an existing brief and ask for improvements:

```
Review this creative brief and identify gaps or areas that need more specificity.
```

## Tips

- **Start broad, then narrow**: Begin with high-level project goals before diving into details
- **Use examples liberally**: Include URLs, screenshots, or specific references wherever possible
- **Make objectives measurable**: Every goal should have a number and timeframe
- **Define the edges**: Be explicit about what's out of scope to prevent scope creep
- **Get specific on approval**: Vague approval processes cause delays and rework
- **Update as you learn**: Treat the brief as a living document during discovery
